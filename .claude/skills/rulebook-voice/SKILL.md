# Rulebook voice

The rulebook is a **procedure**: what a player does, in order, to understand how to perform the actions of playing the game, often looked up mid-game at the table. Every sentence either tells the player what to do or defines a term they need to do it.

## Sentences

- Second person, present tense, imperative, addressed to one player: "Shuffle your exhaust pile into your deck." When both players act at once, say "Each player does the following:" once, then continue in "you".
- A step carries its action and its exception, nothing more. What follows from the exception is its own step or a defined term.
- "May" grants a choice. "Must" removes choice. Strike every "should".
- Game terms in backticks, as the rulebook already does (`Stuff`, `Hold`, `Ascend`). One term per thing, used everywhere.

## Structure

- The steps are the section: at most one sentence of preamble, then numbered steps, then nothing.
- A section covers the whole procedure it names, start to finish, at the level a player executes it. Point to other sections by number instead of restating them.
- Each step opens with a bold label naming what the player does: **Heal:**, **Choose a reward:**, **Say Goodbye to Your Stuff:**. The label names the action, never the rule's design name.
- Bold marks labels and the number or word a step turns on (**one** card, a **different** card). Nothing else.
- Voice lives in the label, never in the body.

## What goes elsewhere

Why a rule exists, what it means for strategy, and how to play it well belong in the design map at `design/core-design/`. Move them there or drop them.

Reassurance is the one that slips through. A sentence built on "nothing", "still", "ordinary", "like any other", or "no longer" states what does not happen or what a thing still is. It reads as a clarification and is not a step. Drop it.

## Scope of a rewrite

A rewrite carries the source's rules and no others. A case the source does not cover is a question for the designer, not a rule to write.
