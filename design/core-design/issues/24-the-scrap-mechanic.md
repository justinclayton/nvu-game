# 24 — The Scrap mechanic

Type: ruling
Status: resolved
Blocked by: —
Map: [core design map](../map.md)

## Question

Not a grilling ticket. A finished rule was handed to the map on 2026-08-27 `[you]` as canon, in
`north-vs-up-scrap-mechanic.md`, and this ticket exists to record it and to reconcile it with what
the map already said. It answers a question the map had left open — *what, if anything, leaves a
deck for good* — and in doing so it reverses two rulings ticket 09 made deliberately.

## Answer

`[you, 2026-08-27]`

### The two graveyards

A card that leaves play goes to one of two places, and which one is the whole of the mechanic.

- The **exhaust pile** is temporary. It is per character, face up, and it comes back to the deck on
  ascending. Unchanged from ticket 04.
- The **Scrapyard** is permanent. A card sent there is gone for the rest of the run: it can never be
  drawn, played, or recovered. There is one Scrapyard, shared, at the side of the table.

**Scrap** is the verb: *move this card to the Scrapyard.* It works from hand, from the play zone, or
from a deck. The fiction is physical — a pipe broken over something's head, a device overcharged
until it comes apart, a tool used past the point of being a tool.

The Scrapyard is a **heap, not a zone**, in the sense the Cleared pile already is: face up, unordered,
never counted, and no rule ever asks it a question. That is what keeps it inside the map's
minimise-play-zones rule rather than in breach of it.

### Scrapping in play

A card may print an effect that costs a Scrap: *"Scrap a card from your hand to [X]."* The card named
goes straight to the Scrapyard — it does not pass through the exhaust pile and it does not come back
on ascending.

This is what **Bad Stuff is for**. Junk dealt to you by a room's punishment contributes no stats and
clutters a five-card hand; Red's heavy weapons and Gray's machinery can now eat it for a large,
one-off payoff. Clearing your hand under pressure and hitting hard become the same action.

### Ascending

Ascending gains a middle step. In order:

1. **Exhaust piles are picked up.** Any **Stuff** in them goes back to its pool at the side of the
   table — no Stuff survives a floor by that route. Everything else shuffles back into the deck.
2. **Held Stuff is kept.** Stuff still in a character's hand when the floor is cleared is **shuffled
   into that character's deck**, where it is one more card of stamina for the next floor. It is still
   Stuff: draw it and play it and it exhausts like anything else, and the next ascension pulls it out
   and returns it to the pool.
3. **The Scrap tax is offered.** A character may choose to keep one piece of **Stuff permanently** —
   it stops being pulled out at ascension and stays in the deck for the rest of the run. **The price
   is one card from that character's starter deck, Scrapped.**

The tax is the game's deck-thinning engine and its only voluntary one. A run trades a weak starter
card for a strong scavenged one, over and over, and decks get leaner and better and smaller. They
also get more fragile: a deck is stamina, so every trade is a card of health given up, and the
preserved Stuff is itself spent the moment it is finally played.

### What this supersedes

- **Ticket 09's "no voluntary thinning" is reversed.** 09 declined thinning on three grounds: it
  needs a new zone, declining a reward already thins for free, and under deck-as-stamina removal is
  self-damage that makes you better — ticket 16's Undaunted failure. The first is answered by the
  Scrapyard being a heap. The other two stand as stated and are accepted as the cost.
- **"A deck is not Stuff's entry route" is reversed** for held Stuff and for taxed Stuff. Ticket 21
  ruled it out because a card entering the deck is a heal, and a heal is the recursion that broke
  four games in tickets 01 and 16. It is now allowed on the same footing ticket 09 gave the mid-floor
  reward: judged per mechanic, with no guard rail, because the supply is finite.
- **"Nothing bad crosses a floor boundary" narrows.** Held **Bad Stuff** now crosses, since step 2
  does not sort by kind. It crosses as a blank card in the deck rather than as a curse, and a blank
  card in a deck of stamina is a card of stamina.
- **Ticket 04's "three zones, no discard pile" becomes three zones and two heaps** — the floor's
  Cleared pile and the run's Scrapyard.

### Consequences flagged, not resolved

- **The heal is now on two dials at once.** Held Stuff is +1 stamina per floor per character, and a
  taxed card is a permanent one. Ticket 10's economy sweep was run before either existed and its
  numbers no longer describe this game.
- **The tax cuts against ticket 22's escalation.** Stuff room count falls 9 → 0 across the tower,
  which is the whole escalation curve; the tax converts early-floor generosity into permanent power
  and flattens exactly that curve.
- **Deck growth was left uncapped on the claim that the inversion governs it.** The tax gives players
  a way to grow power while shrinking the deck, which is the first mechanic that lets them escape the
  inversion rather than pay it.

All three belong to ticket 10, which is charged with re-running against these rules.


## Gap found 2026-08-29 — Stuff sitting in a deck at the ascend

`[finding, from ticket 12](12-exemplar-card-set.md)` The ascend above sorts two places: the **exhaust
pile** (Stuff returns to the pool, everything else shuffles back) and the **hand** (held Stuff shuffles
into the deck). It says nothing about Stuff sitting **in a deck** when the floor is cleared.

That is not a hypothetical. `Heavy Pockets` and `Pack Away` in the
[card bank](../prototypes/12-card-bank.md) both shuffle Stuff into a deck mid-floor, and were ruled to
survive on 2026-08-29 precisely because they do. Whether that Stuff is pulled back to the pool like
other Stuff, or stays in the deck permanently, is the difference between those cards being tempo and
being permanent upgrades.

Unruled.


## Raised 2026-08-29 — the tax's name, and whether it is net zero

Two questions from [ticket 12](12-exemplar-card-set.md), neither ruled.

**The name.** The ascend's third step was described as needing "an official name". It already has one
here — **the Scrap tax** — but that was the agent's coinage while recording the rule, not a chosen
term. Confirming it or replacing it is open.

**Net −1 or net 0.** The section above calls the tax the game's deck-thinning engine and says decks
"get leaner and better and smaller". That is only true if the Stuff being kept was already in the deck
from step 2, so the Scrapped starter is a straight subtraction. If instead the tax *adds* the Stuff
while removing the starter, deck size is unchanged and the tax buys quality, not leanness.

`[you, ticket 12]` described thinning as happening "only in a swap sense", which points at net 0 — but
the two readings differ on whether a player can ever escape ticket 04's inversion by shrinking, which
is the question ticket 09 cared most about.
