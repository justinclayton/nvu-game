# North vs Up — Game Design Handoff

## 1. What We're Trying to Accomplish

Design a **1–2 player cooperative roguelike deck-building card/board game** with a strong narrative component.

The intended experience combines:

- **Slay the Spire-style deckbuilding and run-based progression**
- A **tower ascent** structure, with challenges distributed across floors
- **Meaningful card synergies and deck evolution**
- A novel mechanic where **cards also function as one or more additional resources**
- **Multiple ways to resolve encounters**, rather than combat always ending in simply defeating an enemy
- **Branching narrative** influenced by the composition and evolution of the players' decks
- High **replayability** through randomized encounters, rewards, and story paths
- A deliberately **low-complexity ruleset**, avoiding unnecessary systems and physical components

The goal is to make the game feel mechanically cohesive: **the cards should ideally represent as many things as possible**, rather than introducing separate tokens, tracks, currencies, etc.

---

# 2. Fiction / Setting

### Title
**North vs Up**

### Premise

A small, isolated, militaristic nation—loosely inspired by North Korea—has attracted an alien invasion.

The aliens are **plant-like creatures/monsters**, drawn to the energy generated by the nation's nuclear weapons arsenal.

Players take the role of **low-level military guards** who initially stumble upon evidence of the invasion and investigate.

As they discover the severity and nature of the threat, they attempt to alert their superiors.

The government/military, however, is primarily concerned with:

- preventing public panic
- maintaining the appearance of strength and control
- covering up the existence of the invasion

Eventually, the military may turn against the players.

### The Tower

The game takes place primarily within the nation's **tallest skyscraper**.

The building is intentionally modeled loosely on real-world examples of monumental engineering projects intended to demonstrate national strength despite being impractical or unused.

The skyscraper has never really been inhabited and is now overrun by:

- alien creatures
- alien vegetation
- a gigantic alien tree-like structure that has grown through/taken over the building

Players ascend the tower as the game progresses.

# 3. Decisions That Feel Established

## Deckbuilding

Players begin with a **small deck of simple cards**.

As the run progresses:

- cards are added to the deck
- cards develop increasingly powerful synergies
- cards will likely be acquired primarily through rewards
- the deck is reshuffled when exhausted

Deckbuilding is intended to be a central strategic system.

## Cooperative

The game is designed for **1–2 players cooperating toward a shared victory condition**.

The exact implementation of solo play vs. two-player play remains open.

## Roguelike Structure

Each playthrough should have significant variation.

Randomization may affect:

- challenges
- encounters
- rewards
- available paths
- story events
- narrative branches

The intention is for different runs to produce meaningfully different experiences.

## Cards as Additional Resources

Cards will **double as one or more additional resources**.

This is inspired particularly by the Decipher-era **Star Wars CCG**, where cards could simultaneously represent playable resources and the player's remaining life/deck.

> **RESOLVED (2026-08-12) — see `mechanics-decisions.md`.** Cards are the energy pool
> (activate face-down, convert to hand 1:1, pay costs from the pool), the deck is the life
> total, and combat damage forces permanent exile. "Junk" is the curse analogue — full value
> as energy, dead weight in hand. `mechanics-decisions.md` is the authority on these; this
> section is retained for context.

## Multiple Combat Resolutions

Combat does not necessarily have to end with killing/defeating an enemy.

A possible system:

1. Reduce an enemy to a sufficiently low health level.
2. Choose how to resolve the encounter.
3. **Defeat** the enemy for an immediate reward such as currency/experience.
4. **Recruit** the enemy instead, potentially adding that enemy card to the player's deck.

Enemies themselves may therefore be represented by cards.

This is conceptually similar to the decision involved in catching Pokémon: weaken the opponent, then decide whether to finish the fight or attempt to acquire them.

The exact recruitment rules and balance are still open.

## Gated Narrative Branches

The composition of the players' decks can affect which narrative options are available.

For example:

- Recruiting more military personnel could unlock military-oriented story branches.
- Recruiting more alien-related cards could unlock different branches.
- Some choices may become unavailable depending on what the players have accumulated.

This creates a relationship between **deckbuilding, strategy, narrative, and replayability**.

The intent is that different decks can lead to genuinely different stories.

## Low Complexity

A major design constraint:

> **Strive for a lower-complexity game.**

Avoid:

- too many independent systems
- unnecessary rules layers
- mechanics requiring additional physical components

Whenever possible:

- resources should be **displayed directly on cards**
- or **the cards themselves should function as resources**

This should be treated as a design filter when evaluating future mechanics.

# 4. Other Promising Ideas

These are not necessarily locked decisions, but have emerged as promising possibilities.

### Trading / Bartering

Players could trade multiple low-value cards for a single higher-value card.

Example:

> Give up 3 weak cards → acquire 1 powerful card.

This has two simultaneous benefits:

1. **Deck thinning**
2. **Deck improvement**

This is particularly attractive because it solves two common deckbuilding problems with one mechanic.

Thematically, this could represent:

- bartering
- exchanging supplies
- salvaging
- combining resources
- trading with factions
- sacrificing cards to alien technology
- accessing caches or supply systems

The specific implementation remains open.

### Enemy Cards

Enemies could potentially exist as cards that can ultimately become player cards through recruitment.

This could create an elegant connection between:

**encounter → weaken → recruit → acquire card → deckbuilding → narrative access**

# 5. Major Open Design Question

## What Is the Core Gameplay Loop?

This is currently the most important unresolved question.

Two broad directions have emerged.

### Option A — Combat-Centric

The game follows a structure similar to **Slay the Spire**:

> Encounter → Combat → Reward → Deckbuilding → Next Encounter

Combat would remain the primary activity, with occasional story/events/trading/etc. between fights.

**Advantages:**

- Strong mechanical focus
- Easier to build card synergies around
- Straightforward progression
- Potentially lower complexity
- Easier to balance

**Risks:**

- Tower ascent could feel like a sequence of disconnected fights
- Narrative/exploration could become secondary
- Combat may become repetitive

### Option B — Exploration-Centric

The primary loop is exploring the tower.

Rooms could contain:

- combat
- story events
- discoveries
- NPCs
- resources
- hazards
- choices
- trading
- secrets
- etc.

Combat becomes one type of encounter rather than the definition of the game.

**Advantages:**

- Stronger fit for the investigation narrative
- More opportunities for environmental storytelling
- Better sense of actually exploring the mysterious tower
- Naturally supports branching paths and discoveries

**Risks:**

- Potentially requires many more systems
- Could conflict with the low-complexity design goal
- Deckbuilding becomes harder to design if cards need to handle many different types of encounters

### Possible Hybrid

A potential middle ground:

> **Exploration determines what players encounter; cards determine how they deal with it.**

The tower could consist of rooms/locations, with each room presenting a relatively simple encounter type. Combat remains the primary mechanical system, but exploration determines which combats, events, choices, and rewards players encounter.

This may provide the narrative/exploration benefits without requiring a completely separate exploration game layered on top of the combat system.

### DECISION (2026-08-12) — pinned to the hybrid

Structure is a **Slay the Spire-style map progression**:

- branching path choices across each floor
- a mix of combat and non-combat nodes
- a **boss combat at the end of each floor**

This is the hybrid above: exploration determines *what* you encounter, cards determine *how*
you deal with it. Pinned for now rather than settled — it's a starting structure, not a
commitment, and it unblocks work on everything downstream.

> See `tech-approach-notes.md` and `prototype/NOTES.md` for simulation results that bear on
> this — in particular, a longer multi-node floor makes the deck-is-life clock significantly
> harsher than the 3-fight run that was tested.

> **CHALLENGED (2026-08-12, later same day) — see `mechanics-decisions.md` §1.7 and §3.1.**
> A different structure is now on the table: a floor is a non-linear **floor plan** of rooms
> you can double back through, played as **one continuous combat round** — scramble for
> resources, fight or flee at whim, beat the boss room to end the floor and fully heal. Many
> more, much shorter floors. It is incompatible with the branching-path structure above, and
> is arguably a better answer to the Option A / Option B tension in this same section, since
> exploration and combat stop being separate node types. **Not yet ratified — this pin stands
> until it is.**

# 6. Important Design Tensions to Keep in Mind

Several ideas currently pull the design in different directions.

### Depth vs. Complexity

The game wants:

- deckbuilding
- resource management
- recruitment
- combat choices
- exploration
- branching narrative
- roguelike variability

But it also explicitly wants **low complexity**.

Future mechanics should therefore ideally serve **multiple purposes simultaneously**.

### Cards Should Do Multiple Jobs

This is becoming a central design philosophy.

A good mechanic might allow one card to simultaneously function as:

- a character
- a combat ability
- a resource
- a faction affiliation
- a narrative requirement
- a deckbuilding component

The more functions can be elegantly consolidated into the cards, the less need there is for separate systems/components.

### Narrative Should Affect Mechanics

The story should ideally not simply be flavor text placed on top of a mechanical game.

The current vision suggests that narrative decisions should materially alter:

- available cards
- available encounters
- available story branches
- enemies
- alliances
- potentially the final outcome

---

# 7. Current Design Priorities

When evaluating new ideas, prioritize:

1. **Fun, satisfying deckbuilding**
2. **Meaningful card/resource decisions**
3. **Strong cooperative decisions**
4. **A compelling tower-ascent structure**
5. **Narrative discovery and branching**
6. **Replayability**
7. **Low rules/component complexity**
8. **Strong thematic integration**

The next major design task is to determine the **core gameplay loop** before adding too many secondary systems.
