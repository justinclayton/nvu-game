What this implements

This simulator follows design/core-design/prototypes/12-floor-rules-summary.md, which is the most current statement of how a floor plays. Where the summary and an older ticket disagree, the summary wins.

The turn

  1. Flip — a room into the active room zone. If the draw pile is empty, the Fled pile shuffles back in first.
  2. Draw — both characters at once, each stopping when they say so. At least 1, always. You may not draw up at 5 or more in hand — but the minimum still happens, and that card goes straight to the exhaust pile.
  3. Play — either character, any order. Cost is paid in other cards from that character's own hand. Stats join one shared pool; a Stuff room reads each character's own side instead.
  4. Cleanup — an uncleared room applies its Flee line and goes to Fled. Then both hands and the whole play zone exhaust, except Hold cards in hand.
  5. Turn ends.

Stuff rooms

A Stuff room is its own card, and the item is a different object drawn face down from the Good Stuff pool at the side of the table. The challenge is split per character — Red's line is read against Red's side of the play zone only — so you pay for your own item and never for your partner's. A Down character earns nothing and cannot have Stuff parked on them. The room is Cleared either way and never goes to Fled. Stuff ignores the hand cap on the way in.

There is no step in which anyone chooses whose hand an item goes to. That choice was deleted on 2026-08-25.

Last stand, and going Down

An empty deck is last stand — a state, held for exactly as long as the deck is empty, not a once-per-floor window. While in it every card in that character's hand plays at no cost; their partner pays normally.

A character goes Down when something would Exhaust a card from their empty deck, or when the team Flees a room while they are in last stand. An empty deck by itself is not Down.

Clearing a room while in last stand gets you out: at that cleanup the cards that would have exhausted from hand and play zone are shuffled back into the deck instead, minus 2, which are exhausted as the price. Hold cards stay in hand and are not part of the shuffle-back.

Something the engine surfaced, not a ruling

A Stuff room is Cleared either way, and getting out of last stand is triggered by the room being Cleared. Put together, a character in last stand escapes it on any Stuff room they meet, without meeting anything and without spending a card — they simply shuffle their hand back minus 2. On floor 1 that is up to nine free escapes sitting in the deck. This falls out of two separate rulings and is not obviously wrong, but nothing has ruled on it. Watch for it in the log.

Winning

Clearing the Enemy room ends the floor. Hazard and Stuff rooms are resources on the way to the fight and a floor can end with either still in the deck.

Exporting a play log

The Play tab exports the current floor three ways. .csv is one row per turn — room, outcome, cards drawn by each character, both deck heights before and after, Stuff taken, draws burnt to a full hand, last stand and Down flags — which is the one to open in a spreadsheet. .json is the same rows plus the floor's totals, the settings that produced them, and every log line. .txt is the human-readable transcript, including any notes you typed while playing.

The Batch tab exports one row per floor played across the whole batch, capped at the first 2000 runs, plus a settings file recording the auto-player policy that produced it. Every export stamps the settings, because a log without them cannot be compared to another one.

What this tool does not do

  • Ascending is modelled only as much as a multi-floor batch run needs: Stuff is pulled out, exhaust piles shuffle back, a reward is taken. The reward is a generic card, because which pool a reveal draws from is still unstated.
  • Card effect text is displayed, never executed. Conditional stats, Pack Rat, Faceful of Slime and the rest are read by a human at the table, not by this engine. Stats, costs and Hold are all it understands.
  • Red/Gray asymmetry is only what the exemplar cards print.
  • The reward pool problem from ticket 12 is a component-count question and is not simulated.
